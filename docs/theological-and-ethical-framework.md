# Theological & Ethical Framework

This document outlines the theological, pastoral, and ethical boundaries that govern the design and use of the **Ministry AI Skills** repository.

---

## 1. Theological Foundations

### The Role of Technology in Ministry
Throughout history, the Church has utilized communication technologies—from the Roman road system and the printing press to radio, television, and the internet—to proclaim the Gospel, encourage discipleship, and serve communities. 

AI is a tool of language processing, pattern synthesis, and administrative support. It is **not** an entity with a soul, spiritual discernment, the Holy Spirit, or pastoral calling.

### Biblical Authority
- **Scripture as Supreme Authority**: The Holy Scriptures (the 66 canonical books of the Old and New Testaments) are the inspired, authoritative Word of God.
- **AI is Not an Exegete or Prophet**: AI tools synthesize statistical language models; they cannot receive divine revelation. Pastors and believers must test all things against Scripture (1 Thessalonians 5:21, Acts 17:11).
- **Preserving Doctrinal Distinctives**: Local churches have distinct theological heritage (Reformed, Baptist, Methodist, Anglican, Pentecostal, Presbyterian, Non-denominational, etc.). Skills are intentionally customizable so congregations can specify their theological framework rather than having an AI impose one.

---

## 2. Pastoral Ethics & Boundaries

### Non-Negotiable Human Roles
AI must never attempt to replace or simulate:
1. **Pastoral Discernment & Prayer**: Intercession, personal spiritual counsel, and hearing from God are human callings.
2. **Crisis Intervention & Mandated Reporting**: Abuse disclosures, domestic violence, suicide threats, or trauma care must immediately be routed to licensed human counselors and emergency professionals.
3. **The Sacraments / Ordinances**: Baptism, Communion/Eucharist, marriage, and pastoral ordination are embodied physical acts of the gathered Church.
4. **Authentic Preaching**: Preaching is the proclamation of God's Word through a called, prayerful vessel. AI may assist with historical background, cross-referencing, or proofreading, but the message itself must be born in the preacher's prayer closet.

---

## 3. Financial & Giving Ethics

1. **No Manipulative Appeals**: AI skills must never use guilt, emotional coercion, prosperity gospel formulas ("Give $100 for a 100-fold blessing"), or high-pressure tactics.
2. **Joyful & Transparent Stewardship**: Giving communications must reflect biblical principles of cheerful, voluntary generosity (2 Corinthians 9:7) and financial transparency.

---

## 4. Truthfulness & Hallucination Prevention

Large Language Models (LLMs) can fabricate citations, historical anecdotes, or scripture references ("hallucination").

- Every skill prompt includes instructions to cite accurately or note when an illustration is hypothetical.
- Users are instructed to verify chapter and verse numbers before printing or projecting them.

---

## 5. Summary Table: AI in the Church

| Appropriate Use of AI 🟢 | Prohibited Use of AI 🔴 |
|---|---|
| Brainstorming sermon illustrations | Generating entire sermons to preach without study or prayer |
| Formatting announcements for multiple channels | Inventing fictional quotes or attributing fake verses to God |
| Drafting event volunteer schedules | Offering robotic pastoral counseling to grieving or suicidal individuals |
| Translating sermon summaries into Spanish | Fabricating manipulative giving demands |
| Generating image ideas for sermon series | Ingesting un-redacted member counseling records into public models |
